Pilot test guide for participants

Help us test FaceGate's face check-in

FaceGate is a face-recognition attendance system. We want to know two things: does it recognise you reliably, and can it be fooled by photos, screens or masks? The test takes about 45 minutes on your phone. Every attempt is recorded automatically, so you don't need to write anything down.

1What you need

  • Your phone (front camera) for checking in. Open the link in Safari on iPhone or Chrome on Android.
  • A second screen to show photos and videos of yourself: another phone, a tablet or a laptop.
  • Optional: a printer, scissors and paper for the printed-photo tests.
  • A person who is not enrolled (a family member or flatmate) for the "stranger" test.

2Get started

  1. Open the link and enter the password. Allow camera access when asked.
  2. Enrol yourself: tap Enrol at the top. Type your ID as the Employee ID and your first name as the Name. Tap Capture 3 photos and follow the prompts (straight, slightly left, slightly right), then tap Enrol. Do this in normal indoor light.
  3. Tap Check in. At the bottom of the screen, make sure Secure is selected.
  4. In the Participant box, type your ID. Keep it the same for every attempt, including attacks, because attacks pretend to be you.
  5. Before every attempt, choose what you are about to do in the dropdown (see the table below), then tap Start attempt. The kiosk only scans after you tap it, so each attempt always gets the label you chose.

How an attempt works: tap Start attempt, then centre the face (or the photo) in the oval and hold still. The kiosk flashes a few colours on the screen and shows the result. If the light is poor it will first ask you to improve it, and a borderline result is checked a second time automatically. When it says "Done", pick the next label and tap Start attempt again.

No printer? Skip the four "print" rows; the screen tests are the most important ones.

4Try hard to fool it

The point of the attack tests is to find weaknesses, so make each attack as convincing as you can:

  • Fill the oval with the face, and hold the photo or screen steady.
  • Turn screen brightness up to maximum and avoid reflections and glare.
  • Try to keep the screen's edges or the paper's border out of view.
  • Try different distances and angles, and match the room lighting.

Whether an attack succeeds or fails, just move on to the next attempt. Everything is logged.

5Your privacy

  • Your enrolment photos and the images captured during the test are stored only on the organiser's laptop, for this test.
  • They will be deleted when the test ends. Ask the organiser if you want yours removed earlier.
  • Only use photos and videos of yourself for the attacks, never of someone else.

6When you finish

Tell the organiser you're done, and mention anything odd: attempts that hung, confusing messages, or an attack that seemed to work. If the kiosk asks you to fix the lighting, follow it for genuine attempts; for attacks, try your best regardless.
